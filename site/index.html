Exact size, not a guess

Two-pass ABR spends a byte budget derived from the target size and the real duration. No bitrate trial and error.

Resolution that fits

It walks a ladder from your source size down to 240p and picks the largest frame the bits can carry.

Proof, not vibes

SSIM at three points and VMAF at the midpoint, compared against thresholds and printed in plain language.

Nothing uploaded

Local FFmpeg, local files, no account, no quota. The optional web UI is served from your own machine.

How it works

  1. Probe. Duration, resolution, frame rate, audio bitrate, rotation and HDR metadata.
  2. Budget. Total bitrate = target bytes × 8 ÷ duration, minus audio and container overhead.
  3. Pick a resolution. The largest rung of the ladder that fits the bits-per-pixel target (H.264 0.072 bpp, H.265 0.045 bpp).
  4. Encode twice. Two-pass libx264 or libx265, yuv420p, AAC, +faststart.
  5. Prove it. Sample the output and report VMAF and SSIM with a verdict.

Why VMAF? It is Netflix's perceptual metric and tracks human judgement far better than PSNR or bitrate. It is not magic, but it is a much better answer than “trust me”.

FAQ

Does it upload my video anywhere?

No. Encoding and measurement run locally with FFmpeg. No account, no quota, and it works offline after installation.

Do I need to install FFmpeg?

No. A static build is downloaded on first install. If that fails, halfsize uses an FFmpeg already on your PATH.

Can it compress without shrinking the resolution?

Yes: add --no-downscale. It will keep the source size and spend the budget there, which is usually softer than letting it downscale.

Does it work on Windows, macOS and Linux?

Yes. Node.js 18+ is the only requirement.

Can I use it in a script?

Yes. --json prints a machine-readable result, and --dry-run shows the plan without encoding.

Is it really free?

MIT licensed, no paid tier. If it saves you time, a star helps other people find it.
